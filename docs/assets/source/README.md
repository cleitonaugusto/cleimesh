# Image sources

- `racknitz-1789-turk.jpg`: plate III of Joseph Friedrich zu Racknitz, *Ueber den Schachspieler des Herrn von Kempelen und dessen Nachbildung* (Leipzig and Dresden, 1789), engraving of Kempelen's chess-playing Turk in cutaway, with the human operator hidden inside. It is cropped to the plate and reduced. The source is the copy at the Landesbibliothek Mecklenburg-Vorpommern / Universitätsbibliothek Rostock (2023 scan), marked **Public Domain Mark 1.0**: <https://rosdok.uni-rostock.de/resolve/id/rosdok_document_0000024607>.
- `../fonts/`: Fira Sans Condensed, SIL Open Font License (`OFL-FiraSansCondensed.txt`).

The images in `docs/assets/` are generated by `scripts/readme_images.py`.

Why the Turk: an automaton that seemed to play chess on its own, with a person hidden inside. Don't trust what the machine appears to do; open the box and check.

## Kempi, the mascot

`../mascot/kempi.svg` is an original drawing for this project: a chess-playing automaton in cubist planes, after Kempelen's machine of 1770, with a face seen from the front and in profile at once, a crown with a gold orb and a keyhole on the cabinet door. The PNGs (`kempi.png`, `kempi-avatar-{dark,light}.png`) are rendered from the SVG.
